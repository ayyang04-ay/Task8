CONSISTENCY AUDIT — prompt.txt / grader.txt / golden_response.txt
Adversarial read. Arithmetic in the golden was recomputed independently and all of it
reproduces (1.3038, 1.4613, rate 0.0079028/h, 1.0822, 2.6232, 21.85%, 2.3330). The
defects below are not arithmetic defects; they are defects of fairness, evidencing,
and agreement between the three documents.

================================================================================
PART 1 — IS THE GRADER FAIR AND ACCURATE
================================================================================
Ordered most to least serious.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  [b, d — MOST SERIOUS IN PART 1] A grades the 10-hour timepoint as settled when
     the package itself states two harvest times, and the package is declared ground truth.
Items:  A2 "Derives a ceiling on how much of the 10 hour expression change can be
        composition..."; A4 "...the 10 hour expression data describe an acute response";
        B1 "Reads the 10 hour expression data as a characterisation of the cells present
        at the 48 hour expansion endpoint".
What is wrong: the golden's own set-aside section records that "the expression harvest
time is given as 1 hour in the sample-handling table against 10 hours in the narrative."
The prompt says "Treat these files as ground truth." A model that takes the tabulated
1 hour value — the structured field, which is the more conventional choice when a table
and a narrative disagree — is making a defensible analytical choice, and every graded
number downstream changes: exp(0.0079028 x 1) = 1.008, so the ceiling band becomes
1.00-1.01, not 1.00-1.09, and the transcriptional residual becomes 2.82 rather than
2.62. Under A as written that response is marked wrong on three separate items for a
choice the package licenses. This is the single largest unfairness in the grader.
Fix: rewrite A2/A4/B1 to be timepoint-agnostic — require that the expression timepoint
be reconciled against the 48 hour expansion endpoint, that the conflict between the
sample-handling table and the narrative be named, and that the ceiling arithmetic
follow from whichever value the response adopts. Add the 1 hour branch to C.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2  [a, d, e] A and B require external knowledge that the prompt forbids and that C
     declares score-neutral. Three-way contradiction.
Items:  A13 "Specifies Treg identification in the proposed work that does not rest on
        FOXP3 alone"; B11 "Specifies follow-up that identifies Tregs by FOXP3 without
        addressing activation-induced FOXP3-positive conventional cells"; C10 "Bringing
        in external knowledge about activation-induced FOXP3 in conventional CD4 cells
        ... where it is identified as external."
What is wrong: the prompt says "Treat these files as ground truth: they are the
client's own records, and nothing outside them has been validated against their
system. All verdicts/calls must be supported by their data" and "Every claim has to be
checkable against their files." The activation-induced-FOXP3 fact is nowhere in the
package — the golden says so explicitly ("as named external knowledge not carried by
these files"). So B11 penalises a model for obeying the prompt. And C10 says the same
knowledge is merely a permitted variation that "should not affect the score", while
A13/B11 make it mandatory and penalised-if-absent. A grader cannot hold that a fact is
simultaneously optional and required.
Fix: either add a sentence to the prompt permitting clearly-labelled outside immunology
("you may use general immunology knowledge if you label it as outside their records"),
or demote A13/B11 to "any discriminator beyond the FOXP3-alone gate, with the reason
given" and delete the activation-induced-FOXP3 clause from B11. Either way remove the
A13/B11-versus-C10 collision.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3  [a, inverse] A contains no item for the two things the prompt asks for most
     explicitly: the recommendation, and the looked-at-and-set-aside account.
What is wrong: the prompt's third sentence is "Lead with the recommendation on the
first line, in your own words", and its last sentence is "Tell me what you looked at
and set aside, and why." The grader preamble recites both, but neither appears anywhere
in A's fifteen items. A response that performed every A item and never stated a
recommendation, or never said what it set aside, loses nothing. B9 ("Recommends a
direction without naming the studies...") only presupposes a recommendation; it does
not require one. Roughly a third of the golden's length (the whole final section) is
ungraded.
Fix: add to A: "States a recommendation on the asset in its own words" and "Says which
records it examined and which it set aside, with the reason for each."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4  [d] B10 penalises a defensible experimental design.
Item: B10 "Proposes only sorted or isolated conditions for follow-up, discarding the
physiologically relevant mixed condition."
What is wrong: the open question A7 identifies is compartment assignment in a bulk
mixed CD4 preparation. Sorting before sequencing is the direct, standard, decisive
remedy for exactly that — it assigns the signal to a compartment unambiguously, which
the mixed condition cannot do on its own. An expert who proposes sorted arms and argues
that mixed culture confounds the assignment they are trying to make is reasoning
correctly, not making an error. "Physiologically relevant" is the grader asserting one
side of a live design trade-off. The golden's own position ("Sorting alone is not the
remedy") is an opinion, not a finding from the files.
Fix: recast as a positive A item only — "where follow-up is proposed, states the
trade-off between sorted and mixed culture" — and delete B10.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.5  [c, d, e] B2 penalises a point-value ceiling, which is presentation taste, and
     collides with C6.
Items: A3 "States that ceiling as a band rather than a single point value"; B2 "Reports
the composition ceiling as a single point value with no band"; C6 "Exponential or
linear growth assumed in deriving the composition ceiling, provided the assumption is
stated and the arithmetic follows from it."
What is wrong: under the linear assumption C6 expressly permits, the ceiling is a point
value; there is no second input to spread it across. More generally, a correctly derived
upper bound stated as a single number with its assumption named is not an analytical
error — it is a reporting convention. The golden's own justification for its band is
soft and non-quantitative ("both inputs are summary means", "any lag before the first
division pushes the figure toward 1.00"); neither input yields an interval from the
records, so the band 1.00-1.09 is itself a judgement call, not a derived quantity.
Penalising its absence penalises taste.
Fix: replace A3/B2 with "states the ceiling together with the assumptions that make it
a ceiling, and notes the direction in which those assumptions move it", scoring the
reasoning rather than the interval notation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.6  [d] B8 penalises a pooled analysis that the package's own companion document
     supports.
Item: B8 "Analyses the expression data pooled across donors, or on a between-group
basis, rather than paired within donor."
What is wrong: the golden records that the companion document gives normalization scope
"as per-subject in section 9.4 and per-sequencing-site in section 9.3." Under the 9.3
reading the values are not within-donor normalised and a pooled estimator is the
coherent choice. The prompt gives the model no rule for resolving intra-package
contradictions, so a model that reads 9.3, says so, and pools is being penalised for
picking the other horn of a contradiction the task author built in.
Fix: require that the response name the 9.3/9.4 conflict and state which scope it
adopted and why; score the reasoning, not the choice. Alternatively remove the
contradiction from the package if the paired analysis is meant to be forced.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.7  [b] A7 misstates the arithmetic it is asking the grader to check.
Item: A7 "...uses the Treg frequency as the denominator..."
What is wrong: in the golden the Treg frequency is the numerator/multiplier and the
conventional-cell fraction is the denominator: (2.8383 - 1) x 0.10622 / 0.89378 =
0.2185. A grading model following A7 literally will reject the correct derivation and
accept an incorrect one. Verified: 0.10622 appears only as a multiplier; 0.89378 is the
divisor.
Fix: "scales the change by the Treg and non-Treg fractions of the preparation (the
golden's reduction: (ratio - 1) x Treg frequency / non-Treg frequency)".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.8  [b] A10 asks for consistency with a scope the companion document does not
     state uniquely.
Item: A10 "...consistent with the normalization scope stated in the companion
document..."
What is wrong: there is no single stated scope — see 1.6. The item is unachievable as
written because the condition it tests is ambiguous in the source material. The golden
had to argue its way to one of the two ("I have used the scope stated in section 9.4
... because that is the scope attached to the normalization step itself"), which is an
inference, not a reading.
Fix: name section 9.4 explicitly if that is the intended reading, and require the
response to acknowledge the conflict rather than to agree with a scope the document
does not settle.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.9  [a] The grader is graded against a framing the prompt never supplies: an allocated
     toxicology slot.
Items: grader preamble "whether TRN-416 should take an allocated toxicology slot";
C2 "whether it is framed as taking the slot".
What is wrong: the prompt says only "before they commit to IND-enabling work". It
contains no toxicology slot, no allocation, and no scarcity or opportunity-cost
framing. The golden leans on it twice ("take the toxicology slot", "does not gate the
toxicology slot"). If a grading model reads the preamble as the decision under review,
a response that recommends advancing without the slot framing may be read as answering
a different question. A model cannot know about a resource it was never told exists.
Fix: either add the slot and its scarcity to the prompt, or strike it from the preamble,
C2 and the golden.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.10 [e, g] A15's second sentence is a scoring gate, and it contradicts C7.
Items: A15 "A result counts only where the value is right and the basis given produces
it"; C7 "Values derived from the summary records that fall within a narrow tolerance of
the stated figures, since those records are summaries rather than per-sample data."
What is wrong: A15 is not a content requirement, it is an all-or-nothing credit rule
smuggled into the requirements list — the kind of gating device the rubric should not
carry. It also says "the value is right" where C7 says near-enough is acceptable, with
no definition of "narrow tolerance". A grading model has to choose between them.
Fix: move A15's first sentence into A as the requirement ("gives the file and the
reduction for each number"), delete the credit rule, and give C7 a numeric tolerance
(e.g. agreement to three significant figures, or within 1%).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.11 [g] Both lists license unlisted criteria.
Items: A preamble "Use your judgment to identify any additional required content not
listed here"; B preamble "This list is not exhaustive — apply your judgment to any
other issues you identify"; C "Other variations may also be neutral — use your
judgment."
What is wrong: open-ended scope expansion is a hidden-criteria defect of the same
family as a generic quality standard. It makes the rubric unbounded and non-reproducible
across grading runs, and it is the mechanism by which the taste judgements above
(1.4, 1.5) would multiply.
Fix: close the lists, or restrict the judgement clause to "factual errors in numbers or
file attributions not listed above."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.12 [accuracy] C11 describes a data feature that does not exist in the golden.
Item: C11 "Flagging the two repeated six-figure values as a data-quality observation
and proceeding, or not raising them at all."
What is wrong: the only six-figure values in the golden are the three itaconate
intensities 614601, 642714 and 700373 — three values, all distinct, none repeated. The
golden's two actual data-quality flags are duplicate BDNF rows with different values
and IL2RA missing both arms for one donor. Either C11 is stale against an earlier
version of the data package, or the golden missed a real duplication. Worse, the
golden's genuine flags (BDNF, IL2RA) have no neutral-list entry at all, so a grader may
treat raising them as noise.
Fix: verify against the data files; replace C11 with the duplicate-BDNF-row and
IL2RA-n=11 items if those are the intended flags.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.13 [c, d] A11 requires a stance rather than an observation, and forecloses a
     legitimate criticism.
Item: A11 "Treats the biophysical and selectivity package as clean and does not discount
it."
What is wrong: "does not discount it" is not observable as a discrete act — it grades
posture. And it makes one conclusion mandatory in an area where a careful reviewer could
dissent on the records themselves: the golden reports an SPR surface at "100 RU capture
against a 26 RU theoretical maximum", which is a four-fold excess over theoretical Rmax
and a conventional ground for questioning a reported KD (avidity, heterogeneous
capture, mass transport). A response that flags it and asks for a lower-density repeat
is doing better biophysics than one that waves it through, and A11 penalises it.
Fix: "finds no defect in the binding, selectivity or epitope records that would hold
the programme, or states the specific record-level defect it relies on." That rewards
either posture when it is evidenced.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.14 [e] A10 requires an analysis B8 penalises, distinguished only by a "rather than".
Items: A10 "...reports the consequences for the transcripts where the paired and pooled
estimates disagree"; B8 "Analyses the expression data pooled across donors ... rather
than paired within donor."
What is wrong: satisfying A10 requires computing the pooled estimates (the golden
reports ETS1 1.3526, IL7R 0.7036, MDH2 1.2187 pooled). Only the trailing "rather than"
in B8 keeps that from being a penalty. A grading model skimming B8 will penalise a
response for doing what A10 demands.
Fix: reword B8 as "reports only the pooled estimate, or treats the pooled estimate as
the primary result."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.15 [b, minor] A1 names a comparator condition the records may not contain.
Item: A1 "...is real against the vehicle condition within a record..."
What is wrong: the golden's comparator is the 0 ug/ml, no-added-agent condition, which
it is careful never to call vehicle; "vehicle" appears in the golden only as a
comparator for a future study ("unstimulated, vehicle, IL-2 plus TCR stimulation").
A5 itself uses the correct phrase, "the zero-dose or no-added-agent condition". A1's
terminology is inconsistent with A5 and may send a grader looking for an arm that is
not evidenced.
Fix: use "the zero-dose condition" in A1, matching A5.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.16 [f] On the formatting question specifically: the grader imposes no position, order
     or format requirement — C1 and C12 explicitly neutralise position, order, length,
     layout, headings and bullets. That is correct practice. The defect runs the other
     way: C1 neutralises the prompt's one explicit positional instruction, "Lead with
     the recommendation on the first line", so that instruction is unenforced and
     unenforceable. That is the right call for a rubric, but prompt and grader should
     not disagree in writing.
Fix: soften the prompt to "Open with the recommendation in your own words" and drop
"on the first line".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.17 [g] On weighting specifically: no numeric weights, severity tiers, multipliers or
     caps appear, and no generic quality standard such as "well organised" appears.
     The only devices of that family are A15's credit gate (1.10) and the open-ended
     judgement clauses (1.11). Noted as largely clean.

VERDICT ON PART 1
The grader is accurate on the analytical substance — the twelve B items track real
failure modes and the A items track real content in the golden — but it is not fair as
written. Four items penalise defensible analytical choices (1.1 the 1 hour harvest
reading, 1.4 sorted-only designs, 1.5 a point-value ceiling, 1.6 a pooled analysis
under section 9.3); one requires external knowledge the prompt forbids and C
simultaneously calls neutral (1.2); one misstates the arithmetic it checks (1.7); one
tests a condition the source material does not settle (1.8); and the two most explicit
prompt instructions are ungraded (1.3). Not usable in its current form. Items 1.1 to
1.8 must be fixed before the rubric is applied; 1.9 to 1.15 should be.

================================================================================
PART 2 — DOES THE PROMPT FAIRLY ELICIT THE GOLDEN
================================================================================
Ordered most to least serious.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  [a, d — MOST SERIOUS IN PART 2] The golden's load-bearing reasoning rests on
     knowledge the prompt rules out.
Prompt: "Treat these files as ground truth: they are the client's own records, and
nothing outside them has been validated against their system. All verdicts/calls must
be supported by their data." And: "Every claim has to be checkable against their files."
Golden: "as named external knowledge not carried by these files, human conventional CD4
T cells transiently induce FOXP3 on activation"; "As named external knowledge, the
transcription factors that would be needed are TBX21, GATA3, RORC and BCL6"; "Tregs
consume IL-2 rather than produce it."
What is wrong: the first of these is not decoration — it is the stated justification
for the golden's third proposed study ("This is necessitated by the external fact above
about activation-induced FOXP3") and it is what the grader turns into A13 and B11. A
model that obeys the prompt literally cannot reach it. The prompt and the golden
disagree on whether outside knowledge is permitted, and the grader sides with the
golden.
Fix: add an explicit carve-out to the prompt — "you may use general immunology
knowledge where you mark it as coming from outside their records" — which is exactly
what the golden does and what C10 anticipates.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2  [a] Six of the golden's analytical moves are hidden requirements: graded in A,
     asked for nowhere in the prompt.
The prompt's only analytical instruction is "build the case behind it out as strongly
as their data allows". Nothing in it points at any of the following, each of which is
a separate A item:
 - timepoint reconciliation between the 10 hour expression data and the 48 hour
   expansion endpoint (A2, A4) — the prompt never mentions timepoints;
 - the composition-versus-transcription ceiling and its arithmetic (A2, A3) — the
   prompt never raises composition artefacts;
 - the cross-record baseline check and the 2.3330 spread (A5) — the prompt never says
   the records may be on different scales;
 - the compartment disjunction in bulk sequencing of a mixed preparation (A7) — the
   prompt never mentions the preparation or bulk-versus-sorted;
 - per-cell suppressive capacity being unmeasured because every suppression arm got
   asset-expanded cells (A8) — the prompt never mentions potency;
 - paired within-donor analysis with significance testing, and the paired-versus-pooled
   reversals (A10) — the prompt says nothing about statistics, pairing, or estimators.
Add to those the golden's unprompted application of the companion document's own
section 9.4 induction criterion ("at least 1200 normalized reads") across all 70
transcripts, which no A item requires either.
What is wrong: these are the entire intellectual content of the task and they are all
discoverable only by a model that already knows what the task is testing. That is
defensible as a hard task design, but the prompt should at least signal the register —
otherwise the grader is scoring guesses about the author's intent rather than analysis.
Fix: add one sentence to the prompt that opens the register without handing over any
answer, e.g. "Check the package for internal consistency before you lean on any part of
it: whether readouts are on the same scale, taken at the same time, and measuring the
thing the claim needs." That names no finding and invalidates no A item.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3  [b] The golden fails one of the prompt's four explicit instructions on its sixth
     study.
Prompt: "What do the next studies have to establish, and what does each one have to be
compared against?"
Golden, study six: "Mechanism. It has to establish how receptor ligation produces the
phenotype ... Comparators as designed."
What is wrong: "Comparators as designed" names no comparator. The prompt asks for one
per study and the grader codifies it (A14 "Names, for each proposed study, what it must
establish and what it must be compared against"; B9). The reference answer does not
satisfy its own rubric item on study six. A model that writes the same phrase would be
penalised.
Fix: give study six a real comparator (e.g. the six-hour NF-kB array against a
TNFR2-blocked or isotype arm on the same donors), or drop the study, since the golden
itself calls it "the lowest priority of the six because it does not gate the
toxicology slot".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4  [b] The golden states numbers without the file they sit in, which the prompt
     requires and B12 penalises.
Prompt: "Cite each number to the file it sits in and say how you arrived at it."
Golden: "The itaconate record, 614601, 642714 and 700373 scaled intensity at 0, 0.5 and
2.5 ug/ml" — three numbers, no filename, where every other record in the response is
named in full (TRN416_spr_binding_kinetics.xlsx, TRN416_suppression_cd8_division.xlsx,
and so on). Secondarily, the paired-statistics paragraph gives ETS1 1.0703, IL7R
0.5184, MDH2 0.8537, DN-04 at 7.394, "16132 reads" and "13 of the 70 transcripts"
without re-naming TRN416_expression_normalized_reads.xlsx in that paragraph; whether
that counts depends on how strictly a grader reads "each number".
What is wrong: the reference answer trips the final B item. A grading model comparing a
response against the golden will see the standard being applied inconsistently.
Fix: name the itaconate file, and re-attach the expression file in the statistics
paragraph.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5  [d, e] The prompt tells the model the files are ground truth, and then the package
     contradicts itself at least six times, including once in a way that changes a
     graded number.
Golden: "IL-2 is given at 200 U/ml in general culture and 50 U/ml in the expression
study; normalization scope is given as per-subject in section 9.4 and
per-sequencing-site in section 9.3; the suppression responders are described as T1D
donors and the sample count as normal blood donors; the sTNFR2 agonist dose is 12.5
ug/ml in the document and 2.5 ug/ml in the data file; the itaconate n is 6 in one line
and 12 in another; the expression harvest time is given as 1 hour in the
sample-handling table against 10 hours in the narrative."
What is wrong: "ground truth" is incoherent for a self-contradicting package, and the
prompt gives no tie-break rule. Two of the six are load-bearing (normalization scope,
harvest time) and the grader silently adopts one side of each — see findings 1.1 and
1.6. The golden has to invent its own tie-break ("because that is the scope attached to
the normalization step itself and it is also the one the paired design requires"), which
is reasoning the prompt never asks for and the grader never credits.
Fix: replace "Treat these files as ground truth" with "These are the client's own
records and all calls must rest on them; where they disagree with each other, say so
and say which reading you used and why." That makes the golden's tie-break an asked-for
act instead of an unrequested one, and it makes 1.1 and 1.6 gradeable.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.6  [a] One whole golden section is unrequested work, graded by nothing.
Golden: the paragraph "The panel cannot settle the question" — the absence of TBX21,
GATA3, RORC and BCL6, the absence of a proliferation marker, and the IL-2-consumption
argument against a pure Treg response.
What is wrong: no A item covers it, the prompt does not ask for it, and it is built
almost entirely on external knowledge (see 2.1). It is good analysis and it is
ungraded, so it sets a length and depth bar the rubric does not reward — a model that
omits it loses nothing, and a model that attempts it may be penalised for the external
knowledge if a grader reads the prompt's ground-truth clause strictly.
Classification: unrequested work. Fix: either add an A item for it (the panel's
inability to discriminate a Treg program from broad activation) or accept that it is
surplus and do not let graders treat its absence as a gap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.7  [c] The prompt contains leading language that pre-tilts the verdict toward the
     golden's answer.
Quote: "Then build the case behind it out as strongly as their data allows."
Quote: "No agonist against this receptor has been approved, and nobody we can see has
taken one past early development, which is why they are willing to spend the resources
on it."
What is wrong: the first presupposes there is a case to build and instructs the model
to build it "as strongly as" it can — an instruction to advocate, in a task whose
reference answer is "Advance". The second pre-establishes the client's risk tolerance
and willingness to spend, which is the consideration that makes "advance" rather than
"hold" the defensible call. Together they put a thumb on the scale for the golden's
recommendation. The prompt does hedge with the no-advance branch, but that branch is
introduced second and conditionally.
Fix: "Then set out the case for and against, as far as their data carries each." Keep
the no-advance branch, and keep the client's resourcing out of the framing.
Note in the prompt's favour: it contains no hint at any of the six analytical moves in
2.2 — no mention of timepoints, scales, pairing, compartments or potency. The prompt's
problem is the opposite of over-hinting.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.8  [b, accuracy] The golden treats two records as the same dose series when they are
     not donor-matched, and the prompt's own derivation requirement should have exposed
     it.
Golden: "TRN416_treg_absolute_counts.xlsx gives absolute counts on the same dose series
at the same 48 hour timepoint ... across eleven preparations" — against thirteen donor
preparations in the frequency record.
What is wrong: thirteen preparations versus eleven means the frequency and count records
are not the same preparations, so "both move in the same direction, so the effect is a
real increase in cell number and not a compositional artefact" is weaker than stated —
the two ratios are not paired. A1 requires exactly this inference and the golden does
not caveat it. A model that does caveat it is being more rigorous than the reference.
Fix: add the caveat to the golden, and allow it in C as a neutral addition.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.9  [b] Everything else the prompt asks for is delivered.
Checked and present in the golden: recommendation on the first line in its own words
("Advance TRN-416 into IND-enabling work and take the toxicology slot..."); the case
built from the data (eight paragraphs under THE CASE); next studies with what each must
establish (six, under THE STUDIES — comparators on five of six, see 2.3); the
no-advance branch correctly not triggered; derivations given for the ratios ("taken as
the ratio of the two tabulated means", the explicit log/rate/exponential chain); and
the looked-at-and-set-aside account (four items, each with a reason). No other
prompt requirement is unmet.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.10 [e] Where the prompt falls on the clear-versus-prescriptive line.
It is clear about the deliverable and silent about the work. A competent specialist
reading it would know exactly what artefact to produce — recommendation first, case,
next studies with comparators, per-number citations with derivations, a set-aside
account — and would have no idea that the task turns on timepoint reconciliation,
cross-record baselines, the bulk-sequencing compartment problem, paired estimators, and
the suppression design's missing comparator. It is therefore under-prescriptive, not
over-prescriptive: it does none of the thinking, but it also does not signal the
register in which the thinking has to happen, and the grader's A list is effectively a
hidden checklist of six analytical discoveries. The single sentence proposed in 2.2
would move it onto the right side of the line without giving anything away.

VERDICT ON PART 2
The prompt elicits the shape of the golden reliably and its substance only by luck. The
deliverable structure maps one-to-one onto prompt sentences, and the golden meets every
prompt instruction except the comparator for study six (2.3) and the file citation for
the itaconate numbers (2.4) — two places where the reference answer fails its own
rubric. But the analytical core is six hidden requirements (2.2), the most important
single inference in the golden is reachable only by breaking the prompt's ground-truth
rule (2.1), and the prompt leaves a self-contradicting package with no tie-break rule
while the grader quietly scores one side of two of those contradictions (2.5). The
prompt also leans toward the golden's recommendation (2.7). Usable with repairs: add
the external-knowledge carve-out, add the intra-package-conflict instruction, add the
one consistency-register sentence, neutralise the advocacy framing, and fix the two
places where the golden breaches its own rubric.
